This page is the starting point for the internal development team. It should remain readable in under three minutes and contain only the decisions needed to understand, evaluate, and protect the game. Detailed rules and evidence belong on linked pages.

> **Accepted** — **Imprint Zero** — *You know how to fight. Not who you are.*

## Product objective

> **Accepted** — Create and finish a compelling standalone game. The game succeeds through the experience it gives players—not by demonstrating its underlying technology.

## High concept

> **In progress** — Choose from an active crew of original heroes gathered at a shared hub, then run, jump, shoot, and discover routes through a modern 16-bit side-scrolling action platformer. Each character materially changes how the journey is played.

The crew receives clear operational missions to recover stolen technology, people, and memories from sealed industrial zones. They know themselves as soldiers or marines and retain the conditioning needed to fight, but know from the opening that their personal histories are missing or unreliable. They do not know whether they are brainwashed originals, clones, manufactured people, or something else. Each deployment produces a genuine local benefit—rescuing people, restoring infrastructure, or recovering something needed—while unknowingly rebuilding an industrial identity-control system. The recovered infrastructure, archives, biological material, and expertise could let the authority behind the Missions extract, edit, copy, and deploy personhood at scale. Deployments expose contradictions in the crew's identities, orders, and larger purpose.

The world draws from late-20th-century cyberpunk: industrial decay, technological power, manufactured identity, memory, and institutional control rather than contemporary neon consumer spectacle.

Recovered **Memory Imprints** are both evidence and progression: each reveals truth about an identity, institution, or place while granting a technique, tactical understanding, interaction, or route permission. Imprints expand a character without replacing that character's innate combat identity.

The **authored campaign** opens directly in the Freight Terminal with preselected ROOK, a rifle, and a Mission. First success introduces the Hub; later deployments begin there with character, loadout, and destination choice. Shortcuts and death recovery support the campaign, but procedural generation and randomized runs do not define it.

> **Accepted** — The first release targets PC and is strictly single-player, with one locally controlled character per deployment. Gamepad defines the action design; fully remappable keyboard controls are required. Multiplayer, console certification, mobile, touch, and mouse-dependent play are not requirements.

See [[Design/Game Vision|Game vision]].

## Player promise

Deliver **familiarity without repetition**: the player immediately understands the crew's operational roles and the shape of the action, but gradually uncovers whether these people ever had original identities, which memories are real, who conditioned them, and what their missions actually serve.

The dramatic movement is **act first, then learn to question**. Obedience initially feels natural because being soldiers is the crew's only stable identity. They are technically capable of refusing, but operational conditioning makes doubt feel improper, unsafe, and unlike themselves. Questioning orders, purpose, or selfhood becomes possible before it becomes necessary.

See [[Design/Player Experience|Player experience]].

## Representative minute

The player advances through a side-scrolling space, reads terrain and incoming threats, chooses how to move and attack, executes under pressure, and repositions as the encounter changes. Platforming, run-and-gun combat, and occasional high-density projectile sequences share the same action foundation. Exploration can recover a Memory Imprint that changes both what the player understands and what the crew can do.

## Design pillars

> **Accepted** — **Familiarity without repetition**, **Character choice transforms play**, and **Discovery changes understanding and action** are the canonical production filters. Each still requires validation through representative encounters.

See [[Design/Design Pillars|Design pillars]].

## Intended audience

> **Accepted** — Players who enjoy mastering readable but demanding 2D action, replaying handcrafted challenges with meaningfully different characters, discovering routes that reinterpret known spaces, and uncovering atmospheric science-fiction mystery through play rather than long exposition.

The game does not promise procedural endless runs, constant randomized rewards, continuous bullet hell, a huge backtracking-driven world, passive story delivery, or retro authenticity at the expense of modern usability.
