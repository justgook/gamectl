## Playable-cast principle

> **Accepted** — The game offers multiple authored playable characters. Character choice changes the play experience materially; it is not a cosmetic skin or minor statistical preference.

Each character should communicate an archetypal fantasy through silhouette, pose, equipment, and motion before explanatory text is required. Familiarity must come from readable original design rather than imitation.

## Selection and deployment

> **Accepted** — The player deploys one selected crew member at a time. The active character remains fixed until death or voluntary return to the hub; characters cannot be swapped during combat or traversal.

> **Accepted** — Returning to the hub allows free reselection among unlocked characters. Death does not permanently remove a character or force a randomized successor.

This makes character selection consequential, preserves character-specific replay value, and turns failure into an invitation to reconsider the encounter through a different play style.

## Baseline character: Rifle Marine

> **Accepted** — The first playable baseline is an immediately understandable Rifle Marine: a generalist with balanced movement, range, and survivability.

The Rifle Marine is the control case for the game. One-speed digital running with quick acceleration and reversal, a single variable-height jump, moderate air control, eight-direction twin-stick firing, optional aim-locking, evading, and discovering routes must feel satisfying without an exotic character mechanic compensating for weak fundamentals.

The baseline has no double jump, wall jump, ledge grab, or air dash. Short coyote-time and jump-buffer windows provide modern responsiveness.

“Rifle Marine” is a gameplay role, not a decided name, appearance, biography, or permanent weapon restriction.

> **Accepted** — The Rifle Marine's innate capability is a combat slide: a fast, low horizontal commitment that passes beneath high projectiles and low obstacles. It has no invulnerability frames, deals no contact damage, and cannot reverse direction during its brief commitment.

The slide uses the common character-ability input. Other characters may reinterpret that input with a different capability.

## Crew and campaign

> **Accepted** — All playable characters coexist as an active crew at a shared hub. They participate in one campaign rather than representing separate timelines or mutually exclusive protagonists.

> **Accepted** — World state, discoveries, and persistent shortcuts belong to the crew. Changing the deployed character does not create a separate campaign state.

This supports relationships at the hub and gives equipment transfer a physical meaning: crew members can store, exchange, or reserve equipment between deployments.

## Ownership model

> **Accepted** — Weapons, armour, consumables, currencies, and key items live in a shared crew stash and transfer freely at the hub.

> **Accepted** — Upgrades applied to a weapon or armour item remain attached to that item. Innate moves, personal skills, and character mastery remain owned by the character. World discoveries, shortcuts, and unlocked routes belong to the campaign.

Equipment compatibility may follow from a character's mechanics, but arbitrary ownership restrictions should not punish experimentation or create repetitive inventory administration.

## Identity and conditioning

> **Accepted** — Crew members know from the opening that their autobiographical identities are missing or unreliable. They know themselves as soldiers or marines, understand their current missions, and retain the operational skills required to perform them.

This is not total amnesia. They can communicate, reason, fight, and recognize operational roles. They do not know whether they are brainwashed originals, clones, manufactured people, or another kind of constructed identity. The ontological answer remains deliberately unresolved.

> **Accepted** — The crew begins with an “act first, ask later” orientation. Soldiering is its only dependable identity. Crew members are physically capable of refusal, but questioning orders, purpose, or selfhood feels conditioned as improper, unsafe, and unlike themselves.

> **In progress** — Recovered people, objects, locations, and memories gradually contradict assigned roles and mission briefings, turning forbidden self-inquiry into necessary investigation.
