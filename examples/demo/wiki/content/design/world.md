## Setting direction

> **Accepted** — The world draws from late-20th-century cyberpunk associated with works such as *Blade Runner* and *Ghost in the Shell*: industrial density and decay, technological power, institutional control, uncertain identity, and the boundary between human and machine.

> **Accepted** — Avoid the contemporary open-world, neon-consumer interpretation associated with *Cyberpunk 2077*. The distinction concerns emphasis and atmosphere, not permission to copy another work.

## Campaign premise

> **Accepted** — An independent recovery crew deploys from a hidden hub into sealed industrial zones to retrieve stolen technology, people, and memories. Its members know themselves as soldiers or marines and retain the operational conditioning needed to execute each assignment, but know from the opening that their personal histories are missing or unreliable.

The crew does not know whether its members are brainwashed originals, clones, manufactured people, or another kind of constructed identity. Each deployment should reveal evidence that challenges a mission briefing, remembered fragment, assigned role, or the institution behind them. The campaign moves from obedient recovery work toward investigation of the crew's origin, ownership, and purpose.

> **Accepted** — The crew is technically capable of refusing orders. It initially obeys because being soldiers is its only stable identity and operational conditioning makes doubt feel improper and unsafe.

> **Accepted** — Missions create genuine local benefits while secretly advancing the controller's larger purpose. The controller manipulates context, target selection, and recovered assets rather than fabricating every threat.

> **Accepted** — The missions are collectively rebuilding a system capable of extracting, editing, copying, and deploying human identity at industrial scale. Recovered power infrastructure, neural archives, biological material, identity records, manufacturing systems, and expertise each appear locally useful while contributing to this concealed project.

If completed, the controller could treat personhood as infrastructure: rewrite memory, remove dissent, manufacture operatives, and assign identities according to institutional need. The crew's exact relationship to this technology remains a central mystery.

## World structure

> **In progress** — The campaign consists of authored locations with optional routes, persistent shortcuts, discoveries, and major set pieces. Wastelands, forests, factories, trains, and industrial lifts are candidate location types, not an accepted sequence.

> **Open question** — Are locations connected as one traversable world, selected from a hub, arranged as stages, or combined through a limited hybrid?

## Fictional rules

> **Accepted** — The playable characters coexist as one active crew based at a shared hub. They act within one persistent campaign state.

> **Accepted** — The crew retains procedural and combat knowledge while autobiographical memory is absent, fragmented, suppressed, or manufactured. This condition is explicit from the opening; its cause and the existence of any original identities remain unresolved.
