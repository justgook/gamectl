## Title and tagline

> **Accepted** — **Imprint Zero** — *You know how to fight. Not who you are.*

The name is treated as the game's title unless a later availability, legal, or positioning review creates a concrete reason to revisit it.

## Working high concept

> **In progress** — Choose from an active crew of original heroes gathered at a shared hub, then run, jump, shoot, and discover routes through a modern 16-bit side-scrolling action platformer. The selected character materially changes how the journey is played.

The crew receives clear recovery missions and knows itself as a unit of soldiers or marines, but knows from the opening that its autobiographical identity is missing or unreliable. The crew does not know whether its members are brainwashed originals, clones, manufactured people, or something else. Deployments into sealed industrial zones recover technology, people, and memories. Each mission produces a real local benefit while also recovering components of an industrial system for extracting, editing, copying, and deploying human identity. If completed, the authority behind the Missions could treat personhood as infrastructure: rewrite memory, remove dissent, manufacture operatives, and reassign identity according to institutional need.

Recovered **Memory Imprints** connect story and progression. Each imprint reveals evidence about an identity, institution, or place while granting a technique, tactical understanding, interaction, or route permission. Imprints expand a character without replacing that character's innate combat identity.

The action foundation combines traversal and platforming with run-and-gun combat. Individual sequences may intensify into dense projectile pressure or large authored set pieces such as a moving train or lift under attack.

This is not yet a complete pitch because the game's macro structure and defining mechanical twist remain unresolved.

## Player promise

> **In progress** — Deliver **familiarity without repetition**. The player understands each crew member's operational archetype before knowing whether a genuine identity exists beneath it. Being soldiers is the crew's only stable self-concept, so obedience initially feels natural even though refusal is physically possible. The narrative moves from acting without question toward challenging orders, memory, ownership, and the assumption that self-inquiry is forbidden.

## Differentiation

The game cannot rely on retro presentation alone. Its identity must eventually be stated through:

- meaningful differences between playable characters;
- an original cyberpunk world and journey;
- Memory Imprints that change both understanding and capability;
- a clear progression spine rather than an equal mixture of incompatible genre structures.

## Intended audience

> **Accepted** — The game is for players who enjoy mastering readable but demanding 2D action, replaying handcrafted challenges with different characters, discovering routes that reinterpret known spaces, and uncovering atmospheric science-fiction mystery through play rather than long exposition.

Nostalgia may attract attention, but the game must remain legible and worthwhile without attachment to a specific reference. It does not primarily serve expectations of procedural endless runs, constant randomized rewards, continuous bullet hell, a huge backtracking-driven world, passive story delivery, or hardware-authentic inconvenience.

## Platform and release mode

> **Accepted** — The first release targets PC and is strictly single-player. One locally controlled character is deployed at a time. Gamepad defines the action design, with fully remappable keyboard controls required.

Cooperative and competitive multiplayer, console certification, mobile, touch, and mouse-dependent play are not requirements and must not influence current encounter, camera, progression, UI, or control design.

## Creative boundaries

> **Accepted** — These boundaries protect the current direction.

- Create original characters, fiction, environments, and assets.
- Evoke remembered conventions rather than reproducing a particular game.
- Do not treat existing prototype systems as design requirements.
- Do not use nostalgia as a substitute for a strong representative minute.
